# Task 4: Environmental Factors

### 1. Determine three different environmental factors to consider in which IoT devices and the sensors will be deployed in. Provide a brief explanation for each of the factors

**Temperature and humidity**

Temperature and humidity are environmental factors that should be considered when setting up IoT devices and sensors. High temperatures can cause overheating and damage to electronic components, while low temperatures can cause batteries to drain quicker. High humidity levels can cause moisture to build up in IoT components which could potentially cause corrosion and electrical faults.

**Physical Obstacles**

IoT devices should remain as least intrusive as possible to ensure that functionality is maintained and that the devices can easily be removed and implemented at other locations. Physical obstacles such as walls and other structures can interfere with IoT topologies and communications such as wireless signals, infrared signals and garnering accurate telemetry from sensors. These factors must be considered as environmental factors when implementing IoT solutions as they will affect IoT performance if not considered.

**Power supply**

IoT devices require a stable, uninterruptable power supply in order to serve full functionality. Power outages, fluctuations on the power grid, lightning strikes and storms may interfere with functioning IoT devices and disrupt communications between devices and endpoints over a wireless network.

### 2. List one impact these environmental factors will have on the IoT devices and the sensors. Provide a brief explanation for each of the impact listed 

**Temperature and Humidity – corrosion and overheating of electronics.**

If it is too hot and humid in the environment where the IoT devices are configured, moisture may build up in the electronics causing corrosion and water damage, and components that generate heat may overheat and malfunction.

**Physical obstacles – Interference with wireless signals**

Physical obstacles such as walls, trees or metal structures, may interfere with wireless communication signals used by IoT devices, leading to poor connectivity and reduced IoT performance and capabilities.

**Power supply – fluctuations causing miscommunication and interference.**

IoT devices may malfunction if the power supply malfunctions, i.e. during a thunderstorm the power supply may short the IoT device, destroying the capacitors. Storms can also interfere with Wi-Fi signals, reducing communication capabilities between IoT devices implemented in an environment.

# Task 6 - IoT security requirements

### 1. Identify and explain four areas of IoT security challenges inherent to its growth.

**1\. Device Vulnerabilities:** The constrained processing power and memory of IoT devices pose a challenge for implementing robust security features. Many manufacturers prioritize functionality and cost over security, leaving vulnerabilities that can be exploited.

**2\. Lack of Patching and Updating:** IoT devices frequently lack mechanisms for regular security updates and patches, allowing vulnerabilities to persist. This absence of updates makes them attractive targets for cyberattacks.

**3\. Interoperability:** IoT devices, originating from various manufacturers, often utilize different communication protocols and standards. Managing interoperability and security among these devices is complex, especially when some lack security considerations, creating weaknesses in the network.

**4. Data Privacy and Integrity:** IoT devices gather and transmit data, but ensuring its integrity is difficult. Breaches in data security can lead to severe consequences, such as identity theft or unauthorized access to critical systems.

# Task 7: Networking, Software, and Installation Architecture requirements

### 1. List and explain three network devices or technology required for the installation and configuration of IoT devices?
1\. **Edge IT:** Edge computing platforms and edge servers can be used to preprocess data generated by IoT devices locally, reducing latency and bandwidth usage by filtering and aggregating data before sending it to centralized servers or the cloud. This local processing capability is especially beneficial for real-time applications and scenarios where immediate insights or actions are required.
2\. **Internet Gateways:** IoT devices often need to communicate with centralized servers, cloud platforms, or other devices over the internet. Internet gateways facilitate this communication by providing connectivity between IoT devices and external networks. They may also perform security functions such as firewalling and access control to protect IoT devices and data from unauthorized access or malicious attacks.
3\. **Data storage devices:** Data storage devices are essential components for IoT installations, ensuring efficient management and accessibility of data generated by IoT devices. Cloud storage services such as Amazon S3, Google Cloud Storage, or Microsoft Azure Blob Storage offer scalable solutions for securely storing and accessing large volumes of IoT data from anywhere. Edge storage devices located close to IoT devices enable local data preprocessing, reducing latency and bandwidth usage while supporting real-time applications. Network-Attached Storage (NAS) devices provide centralized storage accessible over a network, ensuring data integrity and supporting continuous data storage and retrieval operations across IoT networks.

### 3. Determine the three installation architecture requirements for the installation of the IoT devices. Provide a brief explanation on each (Approx. 100 words)

Three installation architecture requirements for the installation of IoT devices are:

1\. **Data Lake:** A data lake is essential for storing and managing vast amounts of IoT data efficiently. It should be capable of ingesting, storing, and processing diverse types of data, including structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data, at scale. Additionally, it should provide data governance features, such as data cataloging and access controls, to ensure data quality, security, and compliance with regulatory requirements.
2\. **Cloud Gateway:** A cloud gateway serves as a bridge between IoT devices deployed in the field and cloud-based services or applications. It facilitates secure and reliable communication between IoT devices and the cloud, enabling data transmission, device management, and remote monitoring and control. The cloud gateway should support standard communication protocols, such as MQTT or HTTP, and provide features for data encryption, authentication, and message queuing to ensure secure and efficient data exchange between IoT devices and cloud platforms.
3\. **Big Data Warehouse:** A big data warehouse is essential for performing advanced analytics and deriving actionable insights from IoT data. It should be capable of processing and analysing large volumes of data in real-time or batch mode, using technologies such as distributed computing frameworks (e.g., Hadoop, Spark) and data processing engines (e.g., Apache Kafka, Apache Flink). Additionally, it should support various analytics tools and techniques, such as machine learning algorithms, predictive modelling, and anomaly detection, to uncover patterns, trends, and correlations in IoT data that can drive informed decision-making and optimize business processes.
